## Exceptional parameters, units, and multiplicities

Positive p and q make p/q positive and q nonzero. Together with p!=q,
this gives s>0, s!=1, s!=0, and s+1!=0. The final Lean proof establishes
these facts explicitly before applying `quadratic_factor_transfer`.
For h=(s-1)/(s+1), the map proof has h!=0 and h^2!=1.

In Q[x]/(P_s), x+s is a unit because
P_s(-s)=-2s^3(s-1)^4 is nonzero. The proof works in a commutative
quotient algebra and uses polynomial divisibility to construct the unit;
it never treats that algebra as a field. The transformed element
w=h(x-s)/(x+s) satisfies T_(h^2), by the homogeneous polynomial identity.
The inverse denominator h-w=2hs/(x+s) is a product of units.

In Q[v]/(g_k), w=1-v^2 satisfies T_k. The denominator
w^2-1=v^2(v^2-2) is a unit because its product with v+1 is the
nonzero scalar 2(k-1). The cleared identity recovers v. The
finite-dimensional subalgebra lemma correctly puts the inverse inside
the subalgebra generated by w, so the evaluation maps are surjective.
Equal dimensions five then give algebra isomorphisms.

`factor_degree_transfer` composes the isomorphism with the surjection
onto Q[x]/(D). This algebra has dimension two. Its generating element
has minimal polynomial of degree two, and that polynomial divides g_k.
The hypotheses are those of a finite commutative algebra; no integral
domain or reducedness assumption is present. Thus split quadratic
divisors and squares of linear factors are covered, with multiplicity.

The excluded value p=q is material: P_1=(x-1)(x+1)^4 has degree-two
divisors. No further denominator exception was found in the allowed
domain. The manuscript's quotient argument matches the formal proof.

## Nonprimitive Gauss transfer and parity

Odd squares are one modulo four, so d=(p^2+q^2)/2 is an odd integer.
The Lean arithmetic proves both this oddness and the exact identity
2d=p^2+q^2. Integer division therefore has the intended exact value. The integral
polynomial is

    F=d^2(v^5+v^4-2v^3-2v^2)+2p^2q^2.

It has degree five, odd leading coefficient, reduction v^4(v+1)
modulo two, and constant coefficient two modulo four.

The Gauss helper clears a rational divisor's denominators, takes the
primitive part of that integral polynomial, and applies the primitive
divisor form of Gauss's lemma. It preserves the divisor's degree and
gives integral factors A and B with F=A*B and degrees two and three.
No step needs F itself to be primitive. This is precisely the extension
needed beyond the earlier coprime-parameter review.

Since the leading coefficients of A and B multiply to an odd number,
reduction modulo two preserves both degrees. Any divisor of v^4(v+1)
whose constant coefficient is nonzero is coprime to v^4, so divides v+1
and has degree at most one. Both reduced factors must therefore have
zero constant coefficient. Their integral constant coefficients are
even, so their product is divisible by four. This contradicts F(0)=2
modulo four.

As diagnostic examples only, (p,q)=(3,9) gives content(F)=81 and
(p,q)=(15,25) gives content(F)=625. Both still have d odd and F(0)=2
modulo four. The general proof above, rather than these examples,
justifies removal of coprimality.
