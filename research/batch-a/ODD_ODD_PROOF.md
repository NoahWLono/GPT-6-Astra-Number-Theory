# Second cuboid quintic: the odd/odd quadratic-factor exclusion

Stable ID: wiki-48707305. Date: 2026-10-15.
Status: the joined odd/odd theorem is kernel checked in Lean, with transitive
axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. Independent review
found no gap in the earlier coprime version of the informal argument.
The formal theorem also removes the coprimality assumption.
Novelty remains unverified. This is a parity restriction directly relevant
to the remaining factor type and is retained as research progress.
The opposite-parity case and the full second cuboid conjecture remain open
in this project. No theorem from a 2026 manuscript is assumed below.

## Exact statement

Let p,q be positive odd integers with p!=q, and set s=(p/q)^2.
Define the monic polynomial over Q

    P_s(x) = x^5 + (2+s)(3-2s)x^4
      + (1+10s+4s^2-14s^3+s^4)x^3
      - s(1-14s+4s^2+10s^3+s^4)x^2
      - s^3(1+2s)(-2+3s)x - s^5.

Then P_s has no degree-two divisor in Q[x]. Equivalently, it has no
factorization into factors of degrees two and three. This includes a
quadratic that splits into two linear factors; irreducibility of the
quadratic is not an assumption. Under

    Q_(p,q)(t) = q^20 P_s(t^2/q^4),

this excludes every even quartic divisor of the second cuboid polynomial
for the specified odd/odd pairs. It does not exclude all other factor
types and does not establish irreducibility of Q_(p,q).

## 1. A rational change of variable

Put

    h=(s-1)/(s+1),        k=h^2,
    T_k(w)=w(w^2-1)^2+4(1-k)(w^2-k),
    g_k(v)=v^5+v^4-2v^3-2v^2+2-2k.

Since s>0 and s!=1, we have h!=0, -1<h<1, and 0<k<1.
In particular h±1, k and k-1 are all nonzero. The first pair of maps is

    w=h(x-s)/(x+s),       x=s(h+w)/(h-w).

These maps are inverse wherever their denominators are nonzero. Direct
polynomial calculation gives

    P_s(-s) = -2s^3(s-1)^4,
    T_k(h) = h(h-1)^2(h+1)^2,

so neither forbidden denominator vanishes at a root of its polynomial.
With s=(1+h)/(1-h), the exact substitution identity is

    P_s(s(h+w)/(h-w))
      = 32h^4(h+1)^3 T_(h^2)(w) / ((h-w)^5(1-h)^7).

All constant factors on the right are nonzero. Thus this gives an
isomorphism of the five-dimensional Q-algebras Q[x]/(P_s) and Q[w]/(T_k).
For clarity, rational expressions in these quotient algebras are legitimate:
the linear denominator x+s is coprime to P_s because P_s(-s)!=0, and h-w
is coprime to T_k because T_k(h)!=0. Their inverses therefore exist by
Bezout's identity. Substitution in both directions and the two inverse
linear-fractional identities establish the isomorphism. This argument
does not assume that the polynomials are squarefree.

## 2. The second change of variable, with all exceptions excluded

Define

    w=1-v^2,             v=(2k-1-w^2)/(w^2-1).

The following exact identities verify the maps:

    T_k(1-v^2)=g_k(v)g_k(-v),

    (2k-1-(1-v^2)^2)/((1-v^2)^2-1)-v
      = -g_k(v)/(v^2(v^2-2)),

    1-((2k-1-w^2)/(w^2-1))^2-w
      = -T_k(w)/(w^2-1)^2.

The denominators are units in the relevant quotient algebras. Indeed,

    T_k(1)=T_k(-1)=4(k-1)^2 != 0,
    g_k(0)=2(1-k) != 0,
    g_k(v) mod (v^2-2)=2(1-k) != 0.

Consequently there is a well-defined homomorphism

    Phi: Q[w]/(T_k) -> Q[v]/(g_k),   w |-> 1-v^2.

The first identity makes it well defined. The second identity expresses
the residue class of v as a rational function of Phi(w), with a unit
denominator; that inverse is already in the image because the denominator
is a unit in the source algebra too. Hence Phi is surjective. Both
algebras have Q-dimension five, so Phi is an isomorphism. The third identity
also verifies the inverse formula on every root. In particular, there
is no lost root at v=0, v^2=2, or w=±1.

This establishes a Q-algebra isomorphism

    Q[x]/(P_s)  ~=  Q[v]/(g_k).

## 3. Why factor degrees and multiplicities are preserved

Suppose a monic degree-two polynomial D divides P_s over Q. Then reduction
modulo D is a surjective Q-algebra map

    Q[x]/(P_s) -> Q[x]/(D),

whose target has Q-dimension two. Compose with the isomorphism from
Q[v]/(g_k). This gives a surjective map from Q[v] to a two-dimensional
Q-algebra that kills g_k. Since Q[v] is a principal ideal domain, its
kernel is generated by a monic polynomial E. The kernel contains g_k,
so E divides g_k; the quotient dimension is deg(E)=2.

Thus a quadratic divisor of P_s would give a quadratic divisor of g_k.
The same reasoning applies in reverse. It uses quotient algebras rather
than a count of distinct roots, so repeated factors and split quadratics
are covered. No separability assertion or discriminant claim from a
manuscript is required.

## 4. Integral polynomial and the parity obstruction

Write

    d=(p^2+q^2)/2.

Because p and q are odd, their squares are 1 modulo 8. Thus d is an odd
positive integer. Also

    1-k=4p^2q^2/(p^2+q^2)^2=p^2q^2/d^2.

Multiplying g_k by d^2 gives the integral degree-five polynomial

    F(v)=d^2(v^5+v^4-2v^3-2v^2)+2p^2q^2.

Assume that g_k has a degree-two divisor over Q. Gauss's lemma applied
to the primitive part of F yields integral factors of degrees two and
three. Multiplying one factor by the integer content of F gives A,B in Z[v]
with

    F=A B,        deg(A)=2,        deg(B)=3.

The leading coefficients of A and B multiply to d^2, an odd integer,
so both are odd. Their reductions modulo 2 retain degrees two and three.
But

    F mod 2 = v^4(v+1).

Every divisor of v^4(v+1) over F_2 with nonzero constant coefficient has
degree at most one: it is coprime to v, hence divides v+1 by unique
factorization. Therefore both A mod 2 and B mod 2 have zero constant
coefficient. Thus 2 divides A(0) and B(0), and 4 divides F(0).

This is impossible because

    F(0)=2p^2q^2 = 2 modulo 4.

Hence g_k has no degree-two divisor. Section 3 transfers this conclusion
to P_s and establishes the stated parity restriction.

## Scope, evidence, and prior-art status

The argument uses polynomial identities, finite-dimensional quotient
algebras, Gauss's lemma, and factorization over F_2. It uses no enumeration
of rational points, no finite height cutoff, and no unproved hypothesis.
The identities are independently checked in `verify_cuboid_maps.py` using
the parent's pinned SymPy runtime. The Lean proof is separate from these
symbolic checks. `OddOddExclusion.lean` proves
`NumberTheoryResearch.BatchA.Cuboid.odd_odd_P_no_quadratic`, using the
algebra equivalences and factor transfer in this directory and the parent's
integral parity lemmas. Its assumptions and conclusion match the exact
statement above. The quotient-algebra argument covers all degree-two
divisors, including split or repeated quadratics.

The pinned environment is Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib commit
`e91b128082cc221d6432a6a5aadcd3243af8e76c`. From the repository root, run

    scripts/with_lean.sh lake env python3 research/parent/cuboid-formal/verify.py

This rebuilds all eight proof modules in a fresh temporary directory and
audits the printed transitive axioms. The batch-local `build_cuboid.sh`
provides a persistent build for subsequent research imports.

The natural missing case is p,q of opposite parity. The constant term
then has a different 2-adic valuation, and the parity contradiction above
does not apply. This note advances that complete factor-type target.
The accepted-solution count remains unchanged.

Focused searches on 2026-10-15 for second-cuboid odd/odd, parity,
quadratic-factor, and Newton-polygon exclusions did not establish novelty.
The inspected 2026 papers state the full quadratic-cubic case as unresolved;
their source text does not state the odd/odd exclusion. Search absence is
not evidence of originality, and this is a common local factor-degree
argument. A reviewer should check older Sharipov factorization work and
unindexed sources before any novelty or publication claim.
